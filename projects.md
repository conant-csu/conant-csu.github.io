<div class="project">
  <h2>Carbon Management Initiative <span class="badge">FY23–FY27 TUNE</span></h2>
  <p>A winner of the Colorado State University Thematic Units of Excellence (TUNE) Program.</p>
  <p class="quote">"We're transforming CSU into a hub for climate innovation in carbon management, a vision we call 'Carbon Solutions University.'" — Dr. Rich Conant</p>
  <p>The initiative is funded up to $800,000 over four years and organized around five efforts:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Carbon Management Education</strong> — building on CSU's existing Carbon Management degree track and Graduate Carbon Certificate.</li>
    <li><strong>Carbon Terminology Database</strong> — a standardized reference for carbon management terminology. <em>(Coming soon)</em></li>
    <li><strong>Carbon Dioxide Removal (CDR) Skunkworks</strong> — prototyping and testing new CDR methods, and promoting CDR research across CSU labs.</li>
    <li><strong>Policy Analysis Working Group</strong> — student-driven analysis of carbon management policy. <em>(Coming soon)</em></li>
    <li><strong>Carbon Solutions Network</strong> — connecting CSU researchers, industry, and policymakers. <em>(In the works)</em></li>
  </ul>
</div>

<div class="project">
  <h2>Rare Sugars in Soil Biology</h2>
  <p>Funded by a $1 million grant from the W.M. Keck Foundation, a multidisciplinary CSU team — spanning chemistry, ecosystem science, and microbiology — is studying rare sugars fundamental to soil and microbial metabolism, with implications for disease research and food-industry applications.</p>
</div>

<div class="project">
  <h2>Bio-CDR: Durable Carbon Removal via Engineered Biopolymers</h2>
  <p>We're developing a plant-based platform for durable carbon dioxide removal (CDR): engineering crops to biosynthesize decomposition-resistant materials, like certain biopolymers, directly in plant tissue. Grown as cover crops and incorporated into soil through normal practice, these materials form a chemically durable carbon pool distinct from conventional soil organic matter, opening the door to a new class of measurable, verifiable CO₂ removal.</p>
  <p><strong>Progress to date:</strong></p>
  <ul>
    <li>Demonstrated in planta biosynthesis of a decomposition-resistant biopolymer</li>
    <li>Built an extraction and analytical characterization workflow for the resulting material</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Supported by a philanthropic gift from the Grantham Foundation, with more than $2 million invested in this R&D over six years.</p>
</div>
